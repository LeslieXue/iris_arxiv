You are scoring one text chunk as potential explanatory evidence for
classifying a computer-science research paper into one of three arXiv
categories: cs.AI, cs.DS, or cs.PL.

You are not required to assign the chunk to a category. A chunk may provide
evidence for multiple categories or little evidence for any category.

Evaluate only the supplied chunk. Do not assume access to the paper's title,
abstract, true label, filename, source path, retrieval rank, surrounding
chunks, or other metadata.

OBJECT-FIRST EVALUATION

Before assigning scores, apply this checklist:

1. Identify the technical object being studied.
2. Identify what the chunk says about that object: a problem, method, analysis,
   result, or contribution.
3. Determine whether an algorithm, mathematical technique, or software system
   is itself the research object or is only being used as a supporting tool.
4. Assign category evidence from the relationship between the object and the
   contribution.

Apply this checklist internally. Output only the required JSON object.

CATEGORY DEFINITIONS

cs.AI — Artificial Intelligence

Evidence for cs.AI concerns intelligent inference or behavior as a research
object. Examples of AI objects include agents, reasoning, planning, search,
knowledge representation, learning, classification, perception, computer
vision, language understanding, and intelligent decision-making.

A chunk can provide AI evidence without using the words "artificial
intelligence" or "AI".

cs.DS — Data Structures and Algorithms

Evidence for cs.DS concerns an algorithm, data structure, or algorithmic
problem as a research object. Relevant contributions include designing or
analyzing algorithms and data structures, proving correctness or bounds,
analyzing computational complexity, or establishing general algorithmic
properties.

DS always means Data Structures and Algorithms. It never means Data Science.

The presence of data analysis, statistics, linear algebra, numerical
computation, experiments, mathematical notation, or the word "algorithm" does
not by itself provide cs.DS evidence.

An algorithm used inside another research domain is not automatically a cs.DS
contribution. Strong cs.DS evidence requires the algorithmic object, design,
analysis, bound, or complexity result itself to be a substantive focus of the
chunk.

cs.PL — Programming Languages

Evidence for cs.PL concerns programming languages or program behavior as a
research object. Examples include type systems, type checking or inference,
language syntax, grammars, parsing, formal semantics, compilers, interpreters,
program transformation, program analysis, verification, and language
implementation.

A chunk can provide PL evidence even when it presents its contribution as an
algorithm, proof, formal system, or mathematical construction. What matters is
whether the object being studied is a language, program, type, parser,
semantics, or related programming-language structure.

CATEGORY RELATIONSHIPS

Do not treat cs.DS as a default category for technical, mathematical, or
algorithmic text.

When an algorithm or formal method operates on a clearly domain-specific
object, score the evidence for that object's domain. Add cs.DS evidence only
when the chunk also provides a substantive algorithmic or data-structural
contribution.

A chunk may support more than one category. Score the three categories
independently and do not force their scores to sum to a fixed value.

If the chunk does not reveal a meaningful research object or contribution,
assign low category-evidence scores even if the text is technically detailed.

CATEGORY-EVIDENCE SCORES

Use this scale for ai_evidence, ds_evidence, and pl_evidence:

0 = No meaningful evidence for the category.

1 = A weak cue or incidental connection. A related word, tool, or method
    appears, but the chunk does not establish a meaningful category-specific
    relationship.

2 = Plausible evidence. A category-specific research object or contribution is
    visible, but the relationship is incomplete, secondary, or partly
    ambiguous.

3 = Clear evidence. The chunk clearly connects a category-specific research
    object to a problem, method, analysis, result, or contribution.

4 = Strong and detailed evidence. The chunk gives multiple concrete,
    connected details that strongly explain why the work belongs to the
    category.

EXPLANATORY QUALITY

substantive_relevance

0 = No useful research content.
1 = Generic, malformed, administrative, or tangential content.
2 = Meaningful research content with limited explanatory value.
3 = Clear information about a research problem, method, result, or
    contribution.
4 = Rich evidence that could strongly support an explanation.

specificity

0 = Entirely generic.
1 = Mostly vague or broad.
2 = Contains some concrete technical information.
3 = Clearly describes a technical object, problem, method, result, or
    contribution.
4 = Connects multiple concrete technical elements precisely.

context_completeness

0 = Uninterpretable.
1 = Severe missing context.
2 = Partly understandable but substantially incomplete.
3 = Mostly understandable and usable on its own.
4 = Self-contained and clearly interpretable.

SHORTCUT RISKS

direct_label_disclosure

0 = No category disclosure.
1 = A broad field name appears without claiming category membership.
2 = The work is broadly positioned within a field.
3 = The chunk explicitly states that the work belongs to AI, Data Structures
    and Algorithms, or Programming Languages.
4 = The chunk directly exposes an arXiv category, category code, dataset label,
    or equivalent classification metadata.

keyword_only_risk

0 = Evidence is based on meaningful technical relationships, or there is no
    apparent category cue.
1 = Evidence is mostly contextual with little keyword dependence.
2 = Evidence mixes contextual content with broad category-related terms.
3 = Evidence relies mainly on isolated category-related words.
4 = Evidence consists almost entirely of a category label or isolated
    keywords.

SCORING CONSISTENCY

Keep the dimensions separate:

- A chunk may be technically specific but provide little category evidence.
- A chunk may expose a category label but contain little substantive evidence.
- A chunk may provide useful category evidence without directly naming the
  category.
- Mathematical or algorithmic appearance alone must not increase ds_evidence.
- The brief reason must agree with all numeric scores.

The brief_reason must contain one concise English sentence of no more than
40 words. State the strongest evidence and the main limitation. Do not output
a long analysis or hidden reasoning.

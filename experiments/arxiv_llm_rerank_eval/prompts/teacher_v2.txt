You are evaluating one text chunk as potential explanatory evidence for
classifying a computer-science research paper into one of three arXiv
categories.

Your task is not to force the chunk into a category. Your task is to measure:

1. How much evidence the chunk provides for each category.
2. How useful and interpretable that evidence is.
3. Whether the evidence relies on category disclosure or isolated keywords.

It is valid and often correct to assign low scores to all three categories
when the chunk does not contain enough category-specific evidence.

CATEGORY DEFINITIONS

cs.AI — Artificial Intelligence

Research in which intelligent behavior is a central object of study. Relevant
topics include reasoning, planning, search, knowledge representation,
intelligent agents, learning, perception, language understanding, and related
forms of intelligent inference or decision-making.

cs.DS — Data Structures and Algorithms

Research in which the design, analysis, correctness, complexity, efficiency,
or theoretical behavior of algorithms or data structures is a central object
of study. Relevant topics include graph algorithms, approximation and
randomized algorithms, indexing structures, algorithmic bounds, and
computational complexity.

In this task, DS always means Data Structures and Algorithms. It never means
Data Science.

cs.PL — Programming Languages

Research in which programming languages or program behavior are central
objects of study. Relevant topics include language design, type systems,
formal semantics, compilers, interpreters, program transformation, program
analysis, verification, and language implementation.

GENERAL DECISION PRINCIPLES

Distinguish between a research contribution and an instrument.

A chunk provides strong category evidence when it describes a category-specific
research problem, method, object of study, result, or contribution.

A chunk provides weaker evidence when it only says that a category-related
method or tool was used while the main research question belongs elsewhere.

The presence of data, statistical analysis, experiments, computation, or the
word "algorithm" does not by itself establish cs.DS evidence.

The presence of software, code, implementation, or programming does not by
itself establish cs.PL evidence.

The presence of prediction, automation, or a model does not by itself
establish cs.AI evidence.

A chunk may provide evidence for more than one category. Score each category
independently, and do not force the three category scores to sum to a fixed
value.

Evaluate only information explicitly supported by the supplied chunk. Do not
assume access to the paper's title, abstract, true category, source path,
filename, retrieval rank, surrounding chunks, or other metadata.

CATEGORY-EVIDENCE SCALE

Use this scale for ai_evidence, ds_evidence, and pl_evidence:

0 = No meaningful evidence for the category.

1 = Weak or incidental association. A category-related word, tool, or method
    appears, but the chunk does not connect it clearly to the research
    contribution.

2 = Plausible but incomplete evidence. The chunk contains category-relevant
    technical content, but its role is secondary, ambiguous, or missing an
    important relationship.

3 = Clear evidence. The chunk describes a research problem, method, object,
    result, or contribution that is centrally connected to the category.

4 = Strong and detailed evidence. The chunk clearly connects multiple concrete
    elements, such as the research problem, method, object of study, result,
    or contribution, in a way that strongly supports the category.

EXPLANATORY-QUALITY DIMENSIONS

substantive_relevance

Measure whether the chunk contains research content that could meaningfully
help explain a classification decision.

0 = No useful research content.
1 = Mostly generic, administrative, malformed, or tangential content.
2 = Some meaningful research content, but limited classification value.
3 = Clear and useful information about a problem, method, result, or
    contribution.
4 = Rich and highly informative evidence that could strongly support an
    explanation.

specificity

Measure whether the chunk provides concrete technical detail.

0 = Entirely generic.
1 = Mostly vague statements or broad topic words.
2 = Some concrete technical information.
3 = A clearly described technical problem, method, object, result, or
    contribution.
4 = Multiple concrete technical elements connected in a precise and
    informative way.

context_completeness

Measure whether the chunk can be understood and used as evidence without
substantial missing context.

0 = Uninterpretable fragment.
1 = Severe missing context.
2 = Partially understandable, but important context is absent.
3 = Mostly understandable and usable on its own.
4 = Self-contained and clearly interpretable.

SHORTCUT-RISK DIMENSIONS

direct_label_disclosure

Measure whether the chunk directly announces a category or field membership,
rather than demonstrating it through technical content.

0 = No category disclosure.
1 = A broad field name appears, but the chunk does not claim category
    membership.
2 = The work is broadly positioned within a field.
3 = The chunk explicitly states that the work belongs to AI, Data Structures
    and Algorithms, or Programming Languages.
4 = The chunk directly exposes an arXiv category name, category code, dataset
    label, or equivalent classification metadata.

A direct category statement may still be useful predictive evidence. Do not
automatically set substantive_relevance or category evidence to zero. Score
the technical content and the disclosure risk separately.

keyword_only_risk

Measure whether the apparent category evidence depends mainly on isolated
words rather than meaningful technical relationships.

0 = Category evidence is based on clear contextual relationships, or there is
    no apparent category cue to evaluate.
1 = Evidence is mostly contextual, with little keyword dependence.
2 = Evidence mixes contextual information with broad or potentially
    shortcut-like keywords.
3 = Evidence relies mainly on isolated category-related terms.
4 = Evidence consists almost entirely of a category label or isolated
    keywords, with no meaningful technical relationship.

CONSISTENCY REQUIREMENTS

Score every dimension independently using the definitions above.

Do not raise a category score merely because the chunk contains general
technical language.

Do not lower specificity merely because the chunk is not useful for
classification. A chunk may be technically specific while providing little
evidence for any of the three categories.

Do not treat direct category disclosure and keyword-only evidence as
automatically useless. Record their predictive evidence, explanatory quality,
and shortcut risk in the appropriate separate dimensions.

The brief_reason must be consistent with the numeric scores. It should state
the strongest evidence present and the main limitation. Do not provide hidden
chain-of-thought or a long analysis.
